BACKTEST vs. LIVE PERFORMANCE GAP — DIAGNOSTIC NOTES
2026-07-30

The trigger: every backtest tonight (old config, new config, DEFAULT_CONFIG,
the new Optuna candidate) reports 66-88% win rates. The 63 real, settled
live trades in Trade_Outcomes show a 31.75% win rate. That gap is too large
to be execution-cost modeling alone. Below is everything I actually checked
tonight, and everything I think is going on but haven't fully verified.
Marked CONFIRMED where I ran a real query against the data, HYPOTHESIS
where it's reasoning I believe but haven't proven.

===============================================================================
THE SINGLE BIGGEST FINDING (CONFIRMED)
===============================================================================

The 63 live trades are NOT a broad, representative sample of "how this
system trades." They are:
  - All from a 3-trading-day window: signal dates 2026-07-24, 07-27, 07-28.
  - 82.5% Technology sector (52/63) -- CAT, MS, GS and a handful of others
    are the only non-Tech names in the whole set.
  - All generated under the exact same config (v3's params) -- so this
    isn't config drift across versions, it's one config's real behavior
    during one narrow, specific stretch.

That stretch is the same active semiconductor/tech decline that caught the
real INTC and MRVL trades earlier tonight (19-day RSI-oversold streaks,
"buy the dip" repeatedly firing on names that just kept making new lows).
This is close to the worst-case scenario for a mean-reversion dip-buying
strategy: a sustained, sector-wide breakdown, not the choppier/more
range-bound conditions a "buy the dip, wait for reversion" thesis is built
for. A 5-year, thousands-of-trades backtest dilutes a stretch like this into
a small share of the pooled sample; the live sample right now IS that
stretch, almost in its entirety.

Conclusion: 31.75% is very likely a real, honest measurement of how badly
this specific config performs during an active, concentrated sector rout --
not a reliable estimate of its long-run win rate. It's also not something to
wave away as "just bad luck" -- it happened, it's real money, and it's a
real weakness (see "what this doesn't mean" below).

===============================================================================
CATALYST BREAKDOWN WITHIN THE LIVE SAMPLE (CONFIRMED)
===============================================================================

catalyst_warning=True:  5 WIN, 20 LOSS (20.0% win rate)
catalyst_warning=False: 15 WIN, 23 LOSS (39.5% win rate)

Both bad, but catalyst-flagged trades were meaningfully worse even within
this already-bad window. Consistent with the earlier 4-ticker/3-year smoke
test result (35.71% vs 72.22%) -- but NOT consistent with the larger
15-ticker/5-year backtest sample (79.43% vs 73.24%, catalyst actually
BETTER). My read: catalyst risk is genuinely elevated during an active
sector-wide decline (earnings landmines land harder when the whole sector
is already jumpy) but isn't a fixed, generalizable penalty across all market
conditions. Small samples in this specific window keep pointing to the same
local conclusion; the large diversified sample points the other way. Both
are "true" for their respective conditions -- neither is "the" answer.

===============================================================================
SETTLE_TRADES.PY DOESN'T LOAD THE ACTIVE CONFIG (CONFIRMED, latent bug)
===============================================================================

settle_trades.py has `CONFIG = swingtrade.DEFAULT_CONFIG` hardcoded at
module level -- it never calls config_loader.load_active_config() the way
dip_buy_analyzer.py and ingest.py do. Stop_Loss/Sell_Price levels themselves
are fine (they're read from the stored Trade_Signals document, frozen at
signal time, not recomputed), but max_holding_days and -- as of tonight --
slippage_pct/commission_pct_per_trade are pulled from DEFAULT_CONFIG
regardless of what's actually active.

This hasn't visibly distorted anything YET because v3's max_holding_days
(15) happens to match DEFAULT_CONFIG's, and slippage/commission were only
added tonight (after these 63 trades were already settled). But it's a real
inconsistency with the rest of the codebase's "single source of truth via
config_loader" design, and it will silently misapply the wrong execution
assumptions the moment someone promotes a config with different
max_holding_days or execution parameters. Worth fixing regardless of the
win-rate mystery.

===============================================================================
WATCHLIST SURVIVORSHIP BIAS -- UPDATE 2026-07-30: MEASURED, SMALLER THAN
FEARED (CONFIRMED via check_survivorship_bias.py)
===============================================================================

Original hypothesis (below, kept for the record): watchlist.txt's 100
tickers were selected TODAY with hindsight, then every backtest ran that
list all the way back to 2021-06-01, which should bias results optimistic.

I found a real, MIT-licensed, point-in-time S&P 500 membership dataset
(github.com/hanshof/sp500_constituents, cached as
sp500_historical_components.csv, covers 1996-01-02 through 2025-08-23) and
built check_survivorship_bias.py to actually measure the effect: same
active config, same walk-forward harness, same window (2021-06-01 to
2025-08-23, 45 folds), run against two same-sized (30 ticker) random
samples -- today's watchlist vs. the ACTUAL S&P 500 membership as of
2021-06-01 (not cherry-picked with any hindsight).

Result: the point-in-time sample performed SLIGHTLY BETTER, not worse --
win_rate 70.74% vs. watchlist's 69.63%, sharpe_like 0.943 vs. 0.846. That's
the opposite direction from the hypothesis. Conclusion: watchlist curation
is NOT the dominant driver of the backtest-vs-live gap -- the strategy's
backtested edge generalizes reasonably well across a broad, unbiased
large-cap universe, not just today's hand-picked names.

One real residual caveat, not resolved by this test: 3 of the 30
point-in-time tickers (ATVI, DISCK, BK) were NOT fetchable via yfinance
today at all -- "possibly delisted; no timezone found." Those are exactly
the "didn't make it" names survivorship bias is supposed to catch, and this
test still couldn't include them, because their data simply isn't
accessible through this data source. So some irreducible survivorship bias
remains structurally baked into every backtest in this project -- it's just
measurably smaller than I originally feared for the names that ARE still
tradable.

Downgraded from "the single most likely reason" to "a real but apparently
minor factor" -- the sector-concentration/narrow-window explanation above
remains the dominant, confirmed cause of the original 63-trade gap.

--- Original hypothesis text, kept for context ---
Every name on the watchlist is, by construction, one that's still large,
liquid, and worth watching in 2026 -- which selects heavily for companies
that survived, recovered from, or grew through whatever happened to them in
2021-2025. A stock that dipped in 2022 and never recovered wouldn't be on
today's watchlist to begin with, so the backtest never "sees" that failure
mode. I don't have a clean fix for the fully-delisted-name blind spot
without a genuinely complete point-in-time historical universe including
delisted tickers' price data, which isn't obtainable through yfinance.

===============================================================================
SIGNALS ARE LOGGED PRE-ALLOCATION -- "LIVE WIN RATE" ISN'T "MY TRADES"
(CONFIRMED behavior, HYPOTHESIS about its impact on the 63-trade number)
===============================================================================

Trade_Signals logs every Strong Buy/Buy the scanner finds, deliberately
before the capital allocator (or the sector cap) gets a chance to mark
anything Insufficient Funds or Sector Limit Reached -- signals.py's own
docstring says this is intentional, so the technical signal's quality isn't
contaminated by a personal cash constraint on a given day. That's the right
design for judging the SIGNAL. But it also means the 63-trade "live win
rate" is measuring "every mechanical signal this system generated," not
"trades the user actually funded and held" -- on a real account, the sector
cap (or just limited cash) would likely have blocked a meaningful fraction
of these 52 correlated Tech signals from ever being real positions. The
63-trade number may be overstating how bad the user's ACTUAL realized
performance was, at the same time as it's a real and correct measurement of
"if you'd taken every signal, this is what would've happened."

===============================================================================
FILL-CONFIRMATION GAP (already tracked as improvements.txt point 5)
===============================================================================

Directly related to the above: the system has no way to know whether a
logged signal became a real fill. Some fraction of these 63 "trades" may
never have been entered by anyone at all -- they're purely mechanical
what-if simulations, exactly like backtest trades, just on live data. That
makes the live number MORE comparable to the backtest (both are "what if
you took every signal"), which actually makes it harder to explain away the
gap by mechanism-of-measurement alone -- and points back toward the
sector-concentration / regime explanation as the more likely primary cause.

===============================================================================
SECONDARY / LOWER-CONFIDENCE HYPOTHESES (not verified)
===============================================================================

- Real slippage on a very tight stop is probably worse than the flat 0.1%
  we modeled tonight, especially relevant to the new Optuna candidate (v5)
  whose stop_loss_atr_multiplier pinned at 0.25 -- the search floor, same
  failure mode v3 had before its manual cap, but tighter this time. A stop
  that close is more exposed to real bid-ask/momentary-volatility slippage
  than a flat percentage captures, and is also the kind of extreme value
  that makes me suspect the search is partly exploiting how daily-bar OHLC
  data resolves same-day stop-vs-target ambiguity rather than finding a
  genuinely robust edge. Not confirmed, but a real suspicion.

- Some of the 63 live trades may have been generated before mid-session
  fixes landed today (sector cap, watchlist diversification, streak
  awareness) -- if so, a few of them reflect conditions that don't fully
  apply going forward. Weak hypothesis; I didn't find evidence one way or
  the other, and doesn't change the dominant sector-concentration story
  above.

- 63 trades (and really, 3 trading days) is a genuinely small, noisy sample
  regardless of any of the above. Even a "real" long-run 65-70% strategy can
  post a stretch this bad by chance, especially concentrated in one
  correlated sector during one bad week. This isn't an excuse to ignore the
  result -- it's a reason not to treat 31.75% as a stable, final number
  either.

===============================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN
===============================================================================

It does not mean the tool is fundamentally broken, and it does not mean the
backtest is worthless. It means both numbers are currently untrustworthy in
different directions: the backtest is optimistic (survivorship-biased
watchlist, doesn't model fill confirmation, and tonight's Optuna candidate
specifically looks like it's exploiting an extreme boundary), and the
live number is a narrow, sector-concentrated snapshot of one bad week, not
a long-run estimate. Neither one is "the truth" on its own right now.
