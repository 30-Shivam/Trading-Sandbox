=== DONE ===

1. [DONE] Portfolio-level risk. Was: nothing capped sector concentration or total open exposure -- five Strong Buy signals in one sector were one concentrated bet wearing five tickers, not five independent ones. Fixed: allocate_capital() caps any one sector at max_sector_allocation_pct (default 40%) of your WHOLE portfolio value (today's cash + Current Holdings, not just today's cash pool in isolation) via sector_lookup + existing_holdings. Watchlist diversified from ~89% Technology to 100 tickers across 11 sectors so the cap has real supply to work with. Current Holdings is manually maintained (TICKER,AMOUNT[,AVG_COST] in the sidebar, persisted to MongoDB's Current_Holdings) rather than inferred from unsettled signals, since a logged signal doesn't guarantee an actual fill. AVG_COST also powers a new "Position Review" table -- the same ATR-based stop/target math anchored to your real entry price, with a HOLD / SELL (stop breached) / SELL (target hit) call. A sidebar toggle lets you bypass cash/holdings entirely and just screen raw signals when you want that.

2. [DONE] Backtest realism. Was: catalyst/earnings timing wasn't simulated (so the "69% win rate" number never saw the volatility real Catalyst_Warning trades have), and there was no slippage/commission modeling. Fixed: settle_trade() now applies config.slippage_pct (0.1% default, only on stop_hit_intraday fills -- gap fills already use the real Open, target fills are limits that can't legitimately execute worse) and config.commission_pct_per_trade (0.0 default, % of trade value). run_backtest.fetch_earnings_dates() pulls ~10 years of real REPORTED earnings dates (only the calendar date, never EPS/Surprise, which would be real leakage) so Catalyst_Warning is now honestly simulated (--with-catalyst flag) instead of always False. Genuinely humbling result along the way: a small 4-ticker/3-year smoke test showed catalyst trades performing catastrophically worse (35.71% win rate vs. 72.22% non-catalyst) -- but a larger 15-ticker/5-year sample (457 catalyst trades vs. 14) showed the OPPOSITE (79.43% vs. 73.24%), proving the small sample was just noise.

3. [DONE] Backtest/live trust gap -- full writeup in backtest_diagnostic.txt. Every backtest one night reported 66-88% win rates; 63 real settled live trades showed 31.75%. Root cause confirmed: the live sample was all from a 3-trading-day window (07-24 to 07-28), 82.5% Technology, during an active sector-wide decline -- not a representative sample, not a fundamental break. Concrete evidence this was right: the very next day's settlement run (after fixing the bug below) resolved 23 new trades, nearly all WINs from a sharp reversal, moving the live win rate from 31.75% to 50.0% overnight. Two things fixed: (a) settle_trades.py now reconstructs each trade's own config_snapshot instead of hardcoding DEFAULT_CONFIG, so a trade is graded against the exit policy actually in effect when it was entered, not retroactively reinterpreted. (b) Built check_survivorship_bias.py + sp500_membership.py (cached point-in-time S&P 500 membership data, MIT licensed, github.com/hanshof/sp500_constituents, 1996-2025-08-23) to actually MEASURE the suspected watchlist-curation bias instead of just asserting it -- ran 3 independent 30-ticker seeds (42, 7, 99) after the first seed alone turned out to be premature evidence (same small-sample-flip mistake as the catalyst-warning finding, made twice). Results: seed 42 favored the point-in-time sample (-1.11pp win rate for watchlist), seed 7 favored the watchlist (+4.02pp), seed 99 favored point-in-time again (-1.26pp) -- averaging to +0.55pp win rate / -0.027 sharpe_like, both close to zero. Conclusion: watchlist curation bias, if it exists, is small relative to the sampling noise at n=30 and is NOT the dominant driver of the original gap -- that remains the sector-concentration/narrow-window finding. One residual, unfixed caveat, consistent across all 3 seeds: 3/30 point-in-time tickers were completely unfetchable via yfinance each time (delisted/acquired/renamed since 2021) -- genuinely failed companies remain a structural blind spot no amount of re-sampling fixes. Also found and fixed a real bug along the way: compute_levels() crashed with a raw TypeError instead of a clean RuntimeError on genuinely bad ticker data (a reused/renamed symbol with sparse history).

4. [DONE] Fill-confirmation gap. Was: the system had no way to know whether a logged signal turned into a real fill -- every Strong Buy/Buy was walked forward as if entered exactly at Buy_Price. Fixed: new confirm_fill.py CLI (list pending unconfirmed signals; confirm one with --ticker/--date and an optional --price if your real fill differed from the logged buy_price). Trade_Signals gained confirmed_filled/fill_price/confirmed_at (deliberately NOT part of log_trade_signal's upsert $set, so a re-scan can never silently wipe a confirmation -- verified directly). settle_trades.py now uses fill_price as the real entry price when set (Stop_Loss/Sell_Price never change -- they're absolute levels from signal time) and carries confirmed_filled onto the Trade_Outcomes document; its summary and optimize.py's live-outcomes report now both split "every mechanical signal" from "confirmed real fills only." Real edge case found and fixed: confirming a fill AFTER a signal already settled left the old Trade_Outcomes doc stale (wrong entry_price, missing confirmed_filled) since it wouldn't naturally get re-walked -- confirm_fill.py now re-settles immediately in that case, verified against the real MRVL trade (entry_price/pnl_pct correctly updated in place).

5. [DONE] Correlation-adjusted backtest statistics. Was: summarize_trades()'s mean/std treated every pooled trade as an independent sample, but a real observed failure mode is dozens of correlated Technology signals firing the same day during one sector move -- that's one correlated event counted as if it were dozens of independent data points, understating true variance and overstating sharpe_like's apparent confidence. Fixed: new swingtrade.compute_cluster_weights() gives each (entry_date, sector) cluster a combined weight of 1.0 split evenly across members (conservative -- assumes perfect intra-cluster correlation, zero inter-cluster); new swingtrade.summarize_trades_weighted() is the shared weighted-stats core (Kish effective sample size), now used by both optimize.py's summarize_weighted (combined with the existing recency weight) and by run_backtest.py/evaluate_config.py's reporting (cluster weight alone, printed alongside the raw numbers for comparison). Verified on a synthetic 10-trade correlated cluster: naive pooling reported sharpe_like=1.953 (11/12 wins, wildly optimistic); cluster-adjusted correctly collapsed it to effective_trade_count=4.3, sharpe_like=0.56. Real run_backtest.py smoke test on 7 tickers showed a modest 10% inflation (small sample, only 2 sectors); the effect will be much larger on the full 100-ticker/11-sector watchlist where 30+ same-day Technology signals have been directly observed. Real end-to-end optimize.py run confirmed no regressions.

=== NOT DONE (ordered by priority) ===

1. Automation (Phase 8). This is the practical daily-use pain point: miss a day of ingest.py/settle_trades.py and you have a silent gap in the training data, with nothing telling you it happened. Even before full Kubernetes, something as simple as a scheduled task plus a failure notification (email/Discord webhook on a nonzero exit code) would close most of this gap cheaply. Also directly useful now: more consistent daily scanning means future live samples span more/different market regimes instead of concentrating in whatever happened to be active when someone remembered to run it -- exactly the mechanism that made the 63-trade sample misleading.

2. Alerting. Right now you only find out a Strong Buy fired, a stop got hit, or a scheduled job failed by opening the dashboard or reading a terminal. A simple push (email/Discord/Telegram webhook) on ingest.py finding a new Strong Buy, settle_trades.py resolving a trade, or a Position Review flipping to SELL, would close the loop between "the system found something" and "a human notices," and pairs naturally with point 1's automation work.

3. Total portfolio exposure cap. The sector cap controls concentration within a sector, but nothing caps how much of your whole portfolio can be deployed at once across ALL sectors combined -- a natural sibling to max_sector_allocation_pct (e.g. max_total_deployed_pct, keeping some minimum cash reserve automatically rather than letting a very diversified day still deploy 100% of cash).

4. Risk-based position sizing. Shares_To_Buy is currently a flat dollar amount (Position Budget) divided by price -- the same $250 bet on a calm blue-chip and a violently volatile name. A more standard approach sizes each position so that (shares x ATR x stop_loss_atr_multiplier) is roughly constant across trades -- i.e. risk a fixed dollar amount per trade regardless of the ticker's volatility, not a fixed position size. Would make position sizing consistent with the risk-management thinking already baked into the stop-loss/RRR math.

5. Multi-objective tuning. Optuna currently optimizes a single scalar (sharpe_like). It's blind to tail risk -- a config could win by juicing consistency while quietly tolerating a nastier worst-case drawdown. Optuna supports multi-objective search natively; adding max-drawdown or max-consecutive-loss as a second objective (or a hard constraint) would make "winning" mean something closer to what you actually care about.

6. [BLOCKED, inconclusive] Trade_Score's RSI-streak bias. Real incident: entered INTC (89.60) and MRVL (177.00) off two consecutive days' highest-scored Strong Buys; both later breached stop. Investigation showed both had been continuously RSI-oversold for 19 consecutive trading days -- a sustained decline, not a brief dip -- and this generalizes: checking the full watchlist the same day, 25/40 Strong Buy/Buy signals were in 5+ day RSI streaks, and the TOP 5 highest-scored signals were ALL 19+ day streaks, while genuinely fresh dips (2-4 day streaks) scored lower. Fixed so far: Oversold_Streak_Days / Extended_Decline_Warning (extended_decline_warning_days, default 5) surface this informationally on every signal (shipped, live). Also built: a Trade_Score penalty (extended_decline_penalty_per_day/_cap), wired into optimize.py's 5D search space. NOT resolved: my hand-picked default (1.5 pts/day, cap 30) actually REDUCED pooled out-of-sample sharpe_like on a 15-ticker/5-year sample (0.97 -> 0.892) versus no penalty. A real 30-trial Optuna search across all 5 dimensions ran to find honest values instead -- it found extended_decline_penalty_per_day=3.97 (near its ceiling) with sharpe_like=1.283, but stop_loss_atr_multiplier pinned at 0.25 (the search floor, same red flag v3 had before its manual cap, worse this time), so it was written as candidate v5 and NOT promoted. Blocker is now specifically the boundary-pinning, not general backtest distrust (done point 3 restored a reasonable amount of that) -- would need either a wider stop_loss_atr_multiplier search range or a hand-capped value validated via evaluate_config.py before this is worth revisiting.
