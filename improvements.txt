Context: after three independent rigor upgrades this session (entry-fill timing realism, tune-vs-ticker-holdout, v3-vs-ticker-holdout), the apparent edge kept collapsing toward zero from every direction rather than holding steady -- that pattern (each correction eating into the edge, all converging on the same "thin, near-zero-to-negative out-of-sample" conclusion) is the signature of a backtest edge that was substantially a measurement artifact, not genuine predictive signal being obscured by noise. Reinforcing evidence: v3's rsi_oversold_threshold has been pushed by every Optuna search (original tuning, candidate v5, v7, v9) UP toward 52ish -- barely below neutral (50), not a real "oversold" reading -- and v3 fires constantly (2021 trades on just 27 tickers in one test) at only a 20.88% win rate, staying near-breakeven only because of its asymmetric stop/target (0.5/1.86 ATR), a payoff shape that can look similar with zero real timing skill behind it. Conclusion, agreed with user: pure RSI-oversold mean-reversion is likely near its ceiling -- further parameter search within this same signal is unlikely to meaningfully improve things. Priority shifts to validating whether the CURRENT signal carries any real information at all, then (depending on that answer) adding genuinely different/complementary signal sources rather than continuing to retune the same one.

7. Combining RSI and breakout cohesively -- user asked directly whether the two signals could be used together to enhance results. Answer, reasoned through before building anything: naively running both as parallel/independent entry streams would NOT help and likely HURTS -- the random-entry benchmark already proved RSI-oversold timing has zero-to-negative value versus picking days at random (holdout sharpe_like 0.007 vs. random's 0.1), while breakout has real, holdout-validated positive value (0.167). 9. Agentic/multi-LLM-agent scoring, raised by user as an idea seen elsewhere (multiple agents each analyzing different angles -- fundamentals, technicals, sentiment -- collaborating to a combined buy score). Genuinely worth doing eventually, since it's the most plausible path to the "external data" item above (an LLM can synthesize unstructured news/commentary a pure price/volume rule never sees) -- but real risks specific to this system's whole methodology, flagged before any implementation:
   a. Look-ahead bias is much easier to introduce than with price data. An LLM asked to evaluate a ticker "as of" a historical backtest date may still draw on training knowledge of what happened AFTER that date -- backtesting an LLM-based signal correctly requires genuinely point-in-time historical text (news archives with real publish timestamps), which is a real data-engineering problem, not a prompting one.
   b. Non-determinism complicates the exact reproducibility this project has relied on throughout (e.g. reproducing prior smoke tests byte-for-byte to confirm zero regression).
   c. Cost/speed -- a multi-agent LLM pipeline across 100+ tickers daily, and especially across YEARS of history for a real backtest, is far more expensive and slower than the current sub-second mechanical scan.
   d. The core risk this whole session has been about: a fluent, confident-sounding LLM-generated score is a MORE persuasive but NOT necessarily more valid signal than a bare RSI number -- and the RSI benchmark already proved a plausible-looking, confidently-tuned signal can carry zero or negative real information. An LLM opinion needs the exact same random-entry-benchmark-style validation as everything else before being trusted, and that discipline is harder to apply to qualitative output than to a numeric threshold.
   Recommended sequencing if/when this is pursued: (1) start as a live-only, human-facing context/summarization aid (surface relevant news alongside a signal for the user to review, not something that changes trade timing/sizing autonomously) since that sidesteps the backtesting problem entirely and is useful immediately; (2) only fold it into the actual score once there's a genuine point-in-time text-data source and a rigorous way to validate it beat a null baseline, same bar as every other factor on this list. Not next -- sequenced behind the cheaper, easier-to-validate breakout refinements (items 5/6/7a) above.

=== NOT DONE (older items, lower priority than the pivot above) ===

1. Automation (Phase 8). This is the practical daily-use pain point: miss a day of ingest.py/settle_trades.py and you have a silent gap in the training data, with nothing telling you it happened. Even before full Kubernetes, something as simple as a scheduled task plus a failure notification (email/Discord webhook on a nonzero exit code) would close most of this gap cheaply. Also directly useful now: more consistent daily scanning means future live samples span more/different market regimes instead of concentrating in whatever happened to be active when someone remembered to run it -- exactly the mechanism that made the 63-trade sample misleading.

2. Alerting. Right now you only find out a Strong Buy fired, a stop got hit, or a scheduled job failed by opening the dashboard or reading a terminal. A simple push (email/Discord/Telegram webhook) on ingest.py finding a new Strong Buy, settle_trades.py resolving a trade, or a Position Review flipping to SELL, would close the loop between "the system found something" and "a human notices," and pairs naturally with point 1's automation work.

3. Total portfolio exposure cap. The sector cap controls concentration within a sector, but nothing caps how much of your whole portfolio can be deployed at once across ALL sectors combined -- a natural sibling to max_sector_allocation_pct (e.g. max_total_deployed_pct, keeping some minimum cash reserve automatically rather than letting a very diversified day still deploy 100% of cash).

4. Risk-based position sizing. Shares_To_Buy is currently a flat dollar amount (Position Budget) divided by price -- the same $250 bet on a calm blue-chip and a violently volatile name. A more standard approach sizes each position so that (shares x ATR x stop_loss_atr_multiplier) is roughly constant across trades -- i.e. risk a fixed dollar amount per trade regardless of the ticker's volatility, not a fixed position size. Would make position sizing consistent with the risk-management thinking already baked into the stop-loss/RRR math.

5. Multi-objective tuning. Optuna currently optimizes a single scalar (sharpe_like). It's blind to tail risk -- a config could win by juicing consistency while quietly tolerating a nastier worst-case drawdown. Optuna supports multi-objective search natively; adding max-drawdown or max-consecutive-loss as a second objective (or a hard constraint) would make "winning" mean something closer to what you actually care about.

6. Re-run Optuna under the fully-corrected objective (entry-fill timing + correlation-adjustment + recency-weighting + ticker-holdout, all combined). v3's parameters were tuned before entry-fill timing realism or ticker-holdout validation existed, against a reward landscape that no longer reflects how trades actually fill or generalize. Three independent checks now agree v3's out-of-sample edge is thin-to-negative (entry-fill realism, tune-vs-holdout, and v3-vs-holdout above) -- deprioritized below the strategic pivot above: retuning the same signal further is lower-value than first confirming (via the random-entry benchmark) whether that signal carries real information at all.
