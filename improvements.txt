1. Portfolio-level risk — the biggest gap, in my opinion. Every signal is sized and logged independently, but the watchlist is almost entirely Technology (GLW, ALAB, INTC, ADI, LRCX are all semis/tech). Five "Strong Buy" signals on one day aren't five independent bets — they're one correlated sector bet wearing five tickers. Nothing currently caps sector concentration or total open exposure. This is the kind of gap that looks fine in backtests (which don't model correlated drawdowns across simultaneous positions) and hurts precisely when it matters most — a broad tech selloff.

2. Backtest realism. Two known blind spots compound: catalyst/earnings timing isn't simulated (so the "69% win rate" number never saw the volatility real Catalyst_Warning trades have), and there's no slippage/commission modeling — real fills are always worse than the clean stop/target prices the backtest assumes. Both bias the reported performance optimistic relative to reality.

3. Automation (Phase 8). This is the practical daily-use pain point: miss a day of ingest.py/settle_trades.py and you have a silent gap in the training data, with nothing telling you it happened. Even before full Kubernetes, something as simple as a scheduled task plus a failure notification (email/Discord webhook on a nonzero exit code) would close most of this gap cheaply.

4. Multi-objective tuning. Optuna currently optimizes a single scalar (sharpe_like). It's blind to tail risk — a config could win by juicing consistency while quietly tolerating a nastier worst-case drawdown. Optuna supports multi-objective search natively; adding max-drawdown or max-consecutive-loss as a second objective (or a hard constraint) would make "winning" mean something closer to what you actually care about.
