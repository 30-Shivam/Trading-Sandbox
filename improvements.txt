1. [DONE] Portfolio-level risk. Was: nothing capped sector concentration or total open exposure -- five Strong Buy signals in one sector were one concentrated bet wearing five tickers, not five independent ones. Fixed: allocate_capital() caps any one sector at max_sector_allocation_pct (default 40%) of your WHOLE portfolio value (today's cash + Current Holdings, not just today's cash pool in isolation) via sector_lookup + existing_holdings. Watchlist diversified from ~89% Technology to 100 tickers across 11 sectors so the cap has real supply to work with. Current Holdings is manually maintained (TICKER,AMOUNT[,AVG_COST] in the sidebar, persisted to MongoDB's Current_Holdings) rather than inferred from unsettled signals, since a logged signal doesn't guarantee an actual fill. AVG_COST also powers a new "Position Review" table -- the same ATR-based stop/target math anchored to your real entry price, with a HOLD / SELL (stop breached) / SELL (target hit) call. A sidebar toggle lets you bypass cash/holdings entirely and just screen raw signals when you want that.

2. Backtest realism. Two known blind spots compound: catalyst/earnings timing isn't simulated (so the "69% win rate" number never saw the volatility real Catalyst_Warning trades have), and there's no slippage/commission modeling -- real fills are always worse than the clean stop/target prices the backtest assumes. Both bias the reported performance optimistic relative to reality.

3. Automation (Phase 8). This is the practical daily-use pain point: miss a day of ingest.py/settle_trades.py and you have a silent gap in the training data, with nothing telling you it happened. Even before full Kubernetes, something as simple as a scheduled task plus a failure notification (email/Discord webhook on a nonzero exit code) would close most of this gap cheaply.

4. Multi-objective tuning. Optuna currently optimizes a single scalar (sharpe_like). It's blind to tail risk -- a config could win by juicing consistency while quietly tolerating a nastier worst-case drawdown. Optuna supports multi-objective search natively; adding max-drawdown or max-consecutive-loss as a second objective (or a hard constraint) would make "winning" mean something closer to what you actually care about.

5. Fill-confirmation gap. The system still doesn't know whether a logged signal turned into a real fill -- it assumes every Strong Buy/Buy became a position entered exactly at Buy_Price. A lightweight "confirm this filled" step (even just a manual flag on a Trade_Signals doc, or folding it into Current Holdings) would let settle_trades.py only walk forward trades you actually confirm entering, making Trade_Outcomes an honest record instead of a hypothetical one, and would bridge the gap between "the scanner found this" and "I actually hold this" that Current Holdings currently papers over with manual entry.

6. Risk-based position sizing. Shares_To_Buy is currently a flat dollar amount (Position Budget) divided by price -- the same $250 bet on a calm blue-chip and a violently volatile name. A more standard approach sizes each position so that (shares x ATR x stop_loss_atr_multiplier) is roughly constant across trades -- i.e. risk a fixed dollar amount per trade regardless of the ticker's volatility, not a fixed position size. Would make position sizing consistent with the risk-management thinking already baked into the stop-loss/RRR math.

7. Total portfolio exposure cap. The sector cap controls concentration within a sector, but nothing caps how much of your whole portfolio can be deployed at once across ALL sectors combined -- a natural sibling to max_sector_allocation_pct (e.g. max_total_deployed_pct, keeping some minimum cash reserve automatically rather than letting a very diversified day still deploy 100% of cash).

8. Alerting. Right now you only find out a Strong Buy fired, a stop got hit, or a scheduled job failed by opening the dashboard or reading a terminal. A simple push (email/Discord/Telegram webhook) on ingest.py finding a new Strong Buy, settle_trades.py resolving a trade, or a Position Review flipping to SELL, would close the loop between "the system found something" and "a human notices," and pairs naturally with point 3's automation work.
