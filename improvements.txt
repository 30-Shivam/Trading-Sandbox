=== STRATEGIC PIVOT (new top priority, supersedes pure parameter-tuning) ===

Context: after three independent rigor upgrades this session (entry-fill timing realism, tune-vs-ticker-holdout, v3-vs-ticker-holdout), the apparent edge kept collapsing toward zero from every direction rather than holding steady -- that pattern (each correction eating into the edge, all converging on the same "thin, near-zero-to-negative out-of-sample" conclusion) is the signature of a backtest edge that was substantially a measurement artifact, not genuine predictive signal being obscured by noise. Reinforcing evidence: v3's rsi_oversold_threshold has been pushed by every Optuna search (original tuning, candidate v5, v7, v9) UP toward 52ish -- barely below neutral (50), not a real "oversold" reading -- and v3 fires constantly (2021 trades on just 27 tickers in one test) at only a 20.88% win rate, staying near-breakeven only because of its asymmetric stop/target (0.5/1.86 ATR), a payoff shape that can look similar with zero real timing skill behind it. Conclusion, agreed with user: pure RSI-oversold mean-reversion is likely near its ceiling -- further parameter search within this same signal is unlikely to meaningfully improve things. Priority shifts to validating whether the CURRENT signal carries any real information at all, then (depending on that answer) adding genuinely different/complementary signal sources rather than continuing to retune the same one.

    The technical build is straightforward -- a "chart agent" (rendered price/volume chart image fed to a vision-capable model) and a "news agent" (headlines/fundamentals fed to a text model), each scoring a ticker independently, combined into an AI_Score field displayed alongside Trade_Score (NOT blended into it).

    The hard problem is validation, and it's harder here than it was for RSI or breakout. Every signal this project has ever trusted (RSI, breakout, all its filters) went through the SAME gate before being taken seriously: a random-entry benchmark and/or ticker-holdout backtest proving the signal beats picking days/tickers at random. An AI_Score needs the identical bar before it should influence a real decision -- "it sounds smart" is exactly the trap the RSI investigation exposed (a plausible, confidently-tuned signal that carried zero real information). Backtesting an AI_Score honestly requires:
      a. Point-in-time chart images -- renderable from existing OHLCV history for any historical date, no real obstacle.
      b. Point-in-time NEWS text -- the real blocker. yfinance's .news only returns CURRENT headlines, not an archive of what was published as of a past date. Without genuine point-in-time news, "backtesting" the news agent on history would let it see (or the underlying model's training data would already contain) information from AFTER the simulated date -- exactly the look-ahead bias item 9 flagged, now concretely blocking the news half of this specific idea.
      c. Determinism/cost/speed at backtest scale -- fine for a live scan (a few signals a day), a real obstacle for re-running years of history across many trials the way Optuna does today.

    Recommended path if this gets picked up: run AI_Score LIVE-ONLY alongside Trade_Score for real time going forward (log both to MongoDB on every scan, not just Strong Buy/Buy), accumulating genuine forward-looking data -- effectively "paper trading" the AI score. After enough elapsed time/samples, apply the SAME random-entry-style validation this project has used for every other signal (does AI_Score's ranking predict real subsequent outcomes better than a random ranking?) before ever considering it as an input to real Trade_Score or capital allocation. This is slower than building a scored blend today, but it's the only way to know whether the AI score carries real information rather than just sounding informed -- the central, hard-won lesson of this entire session. Not started; needs the user's go-ahead on this design (and ideally a resolved point-in-time news data source) before implementation begins. DEPRIORITIZED for now -- user asked to put the agentic avenue on the backburner in favor of more mechanical accuracy work (see item 17).

18. [DONE, inconclusive -- nominal win on a sample too thin to trust] On-Balance Volume (OBV) accumulation z-score and Bollinger Band volatility-squeeze z-score, the two complementary indicators the user asked for by name after ADX came up empty. Both target dimensions ADX/RSI/relative-strength/volume-ratio don't cover: OBV_Zscore reads sustained buying pressure BUILDING UP over weeks (not a single day's volume spike like Volume_Ratio), Squeeze_Zscore reads whether volatility was unusually CONTRACTED right before the breakout (the classic "coiled spring" pattern) -- a volatility-regime signal distinct from momentum, direction, trend-strength, or volume.

    Design notes worth recording: OBV is a raw CUMULATIVE sum (unlike RSI/ATR/ADX's Wilder-smoothing or SMA's fixed window) with no decay at all, so its absolute magnitude is arbitrary -- it depends on how much leading history precedes the window. Rather than use it raw, both OBV and the Bollinger volatility measure are z-scored against their own trailing rolling mean/stdev (config.obv_window=20, config.bb_squeeze_window=60) before gating -- proved (not just argued) invariant to leading-history length, since OBV and its rolling mean shift by the identical constant offset for any amount of extra history, canceling in the difference. Squeeze_Zscore is additionally read as of the PRIOR day (`.shift(1)`) so today's own breakout move can't inflate the volatility reading and mask the squeeze that preceded it -- same no-look-ahead-into-today convention Highest_High already used. New config.breakout_obv_zscore_min (-100.0/disabled default) and config.breakout_squeeze_zscore_max (100.0/disabled default), gated identically in simulate_breakout_signals/add_breakout_trade_score, added as 8th/9th dimensions to optimize.py's search space (both -3.0 to 3.0, spanning realistic z-score extremes). Verified: unit tests on synthetic data (a recent high-volume accumulation burst after a decline produced OBV_Zscore=1.61; a recent volatility contraction after a volatile stretch produced Squeeze_Zscore=-2.53 -- both indicators correctly read the intended regime; both gates correctly zero out a signal that can't clear a demanding bar while leaving disabled defaults untouched; missing/NaN values on either field correctly don't exclude a signal on their own). AppTest clean on both the real active v19 config and a monkeypatched config with both filters actually engaged.

    Full-scale result (152 tickers, 112 tune / 40 holdout, 45 trials -- raised from 30 given a 9D space and the lesson from item 17 that 30 trials struggled to explore 7D well): best trial -- breakout_lookback_days=45 (matches v19 exactly), atr_take_profit_multiplier=2.44, stop_loss_atr_multiplier=4.98, breakout_rsi_overbought_threshold=74.97, breakout_relative_strength_min=-14.95, breakout_volume_ratio_min=0.02 (~disabled), breakout_adx_min=18.71, breakout_obv_zscore_min=1.46, breakout_squeeze_zscore_max=-1.46. Unlike ADX, BOTH new filters landed at genuinely demanding, non-disabled values this time -- the search clearly found them worth engaging. Written as candidate v24. On its own holdout: win_rate=61.84%, sharpe_like=0.481, effective_trade_count=16.1 -- a nominally strong sharpe_like, but the sample is barely above MIN_TRADES_FOR_SCORE's 15-trade floor (the search itself printed the thin-sample warning), an order of magnitude smaller than v19's ~161-trade holdout sample.     Re-scored v19's exact stored params against this SAME new 40-ticker holdout split (same fair-comparison discipline as items 14/17): win_rate=70.81%, sharpe_like=0.248, effective_trade_count=161.1.
